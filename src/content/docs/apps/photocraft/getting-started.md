## 1. Install PhotoCraft

The quickest way is [ArtCraft Launcher](https://github.com/storytold/craft-launcher/releases/latest): install it, then press **Install** on PhotoCraft's card. To download PhotoCraft on its own, see [Install and update](/apps/photocraft/install-update/).

## 2. Open an image

PhotoCraft opens on its **Home** screen. Choose **Open…** and pick an image, or pick one under **Recent**. To start from nothing, choose **New file**, set the size and click **Create**.

You can also use **File › Open…** (**Ctrl+O**, or **⌘O** on a Mac) at any time.

The panels you will use most are on the right: **Properties**, **Layers** and **History**. The tools are on the left.

## 3. Make an edit that you can change later

Try an adjustment layer. It changes how the image looks without touching its pixels.

1. Choose **Layer › New Adjustment Layer › Curves…**.
2. Drag the curve in the **Properties** panel. Pull the middle up to brighten the image, or make a gentle S shape for more contrast.
3. To change it later, click the **Curves 1** layer in the **Layers** panel. To switch it off, click its eye icon.

Every step is listed in the **History** panel. **Ctrl+Z** (**⌘Z**) undoes the last one, and **Ctrl+Shift+Z** (**⌘⇧Z**) redoes it.

## 4. Add some text

1. Pick the **Horizontal Type Tool** (press **T**).
2. Click on the image and type.
3. Change the font, size and color in the options bar at the top, or in the **Properties** panel.

The text stays editable. Click it again with the Type tool to change it.

## 5. Save your work

To keep your layers, choose **File › Save As…** (**Ctrl+Shift+S**, or **⌘⇧S**) and pick **PSD Document** or **PhotoCraft** as the type. Use PSD to share the file with other image editors, or PhotoCraft (`.pcraft`) for PhotoCraft's own format.

PhotoCraft also saves recovery information for unsaved documents in the background. If it closes unexpectedly, it reopens them the next time you start it.

## 6. Export a copy to share

Choose **File › Export › Export As…**, pick **PNG** or **JPG** under **Format**, and click **Export**. The dialog shows a preview and an estimate of the file size first. Your layered document isn't changed.
